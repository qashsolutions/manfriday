Project Viral

A short-form content engine built on the category's proven model: paste a product URL, swipe through trend-anchored video concepts, publish straight to TikTok and YouTube Shorts. This document is the plan — all four decisions are locked and the scope is frozen. Design is next; only after design sign-off does the build start.

source: category research · Sep 2026 source: founder interview, Starter Story (17 min) product: Man Friday · manfriday.app

The reference

The proven model, and why it works

The category leader is a content factory plus scheduler for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts. You enter your website URL; it learns the product, audience, and tone, then generates thousands of short-form video concepts seeded from a library of 25,000+ tagged trending videos. A Tinder-style swipe interface lets you swipe through them — the trending reference video on the left, your generated version in the middle. Swipe right, pick platforms, let AI write the caption, and the post is live within five minutes. Analytics feed back in so you generate more of whatever worked.

$69KMRR, 2 months after public launch (Mar 23)
1,000+paid users; $1M+ ARR across the business
2,000customer calls by two founders, ~20/week
~5 mofrom first vertical code (Oct) to public launch

From the founder interview, four things actually made it win — and they shape this plan:

1 · Reference-anchored generation. Every concept is shown next to the real trending video it copies (e.g. a 3M-view slideshow), same format applied to your product. The user trusts the output because they can see the proof.

2 · Swipe as the entire UX. Curation costs one flick of the thumb. No timeline editor, no blank canvas.

3 · The loop closes in one app. Generate → approve → schedule → publish → measure, without leaving.

4 · Relentless customer discovery. A "book a call, get 7 extra days of access" button, Mum-Test questions, silent usability tests, PostHog power-user tracking, and a customer-intelligence dashboard driving the roadmap. Started horizontal ("do all of marketing"), niched to short-form only after the calls demanded it.

"Most startups don't die because the ideas are bad. They die because founders skip customer discovery."

Razor scope

What v1 is — and is not

The v1 test, in one sentence: a user pastes a URL, swipes through ten trend-anchored concepts, publishes one to TikTok, and sees its view count — all in a single session. Everything below is judged against that sentence. Video formats also cross-post to YouTube Shorts (D1); slideshows are TikTok-only.

INVersion 1

  • URL ingestion → brand brief (product, audience, tone, niche) via LLM
  • Seeded trend library: 300–500 hand-tagged trending formats across 3–5 niches — not 25k; curation is the moat, volume comes later
  • Three formats: photo slideshows, faceless hook videos (b-roll + AI voiceover + captions), and AI avatar talking-head videos from a curated set of ~20 stock characters — avatars render full-res only on right-swipe to contain cost (D2)
  • Picks — the swipe review screen — reference left, yours center — our own name for the category's proven swipe pattern
  • Schedule + direct publish to TikTok + YouTube Shorts (D1) — slideshows are TikTok photo-mode only; hook and avatar videos cross-post to Shorts
  • Post-level analytics + "make more like this"
  • Auth, waitlist, a book-a-call button, and Stripe card-on-file at signup with a 30-day free window for the first 500 customers (D4)
  • Web app only — responsive, PWA-grade on mobile, Picks screen designed mobile-first — matches the market leader; native apps are v2+ only if beta calls demand

OUTDeferred or never

  • AI Studio build-your-own characters + a 1,000-character catalog — v1 ships ~20 curated avatars; custom is v2
  • Meme generator, hook-only tool — v2+
  • Human UGC clip marketplace — a supply-side business, not software
  • Instagram publishing — fast follow once the loop is proven; needs Business-account conversion + Meta app review
  • Public API / MCP, multi-workspace, teams — later
  • Warmed-accounts / follower sales — never; violates platform ToS

The product

Core loop the design phase must specify

One pipeline, seven stages, two feedback loops. Each arrow carries a concrete artifact — these artifacts are exactly the data model the design phase has to pin down.

Two design-phase notes that fall out of the founder interview: the Picks screen must always show the reference trending video beside the generated one (that pairing is the trust mechanism), and the light editor is deliberately not a timeline — swap the hook, the voiceover, or a slide, nothing more.

Determinism first

Where Claude is allowed — and where Python does the job

Principle, locked 4 Sep: every pipeline stage runs as deterministic code unless creative language generation is unavoidable. Claude is confined to exactly two call sites. Everything else is Python in the render worker, or plain application code.

UX guardrail (also locked 4 Sep): determinism is a cost tactic, never a UX tactic. The experience stays cutting-edge — optimize only where the user can't feel it. In practice the two goals reinforce each other: overnight Batch-API pre-generation is what makes the swipe feed feel instant; deterministic Pillow/ffmpeg assembly is what makes previews render in seconds instead of minutes; and the money saved is what funds the wow moments — 60fps swipe physics, live-publish confetti, avatar renders — where spending IS the right call.

Cost levers on the two Claude call sites: prompt caching (stable system + template prefix, per-brief content after the cache breakpoint), the Batch API at 50% off for overnight feed pre-generation (concept refresh is not latency-sensitive), structured outputs to eliminate parse retries, per-user credit caps (already in the product design), and cost-per-concept telemetry in Axiom. Expected LLM spend: well under $1 per user per month. The dominant variable cost is media APIs — TTS and avatar lipsync — which is exactly why lazy rendering on right-swipe is this plan's most important cost decision, not anything about the LLM.

Scope frozen

All four decisions, locked

Locked 3 Sep 2026. Recorded here with their implications; changing any of them reopens the scope freeze.

D1 · PLATFORM ORDER — LOCKED

TikTok + YouTube Shorts in v1; Instagram is the fast follow. YouTube's Data API is openly available, so the second platform is cheap to add — OAuth + upload is far simpler than Instagram's Business-account-conversion + Meta-review path. Three implications the design phase carries: (1) slideshows are TikTok photo-mode only — Shorts gets hook and avatar videos, and whether slideshows auto-convert to video montages for Shorts is a design-phase call; (2) YouTube's quota increase and TikTok's audit both get applied for during design (see risks); (3) the metrics loop normalizes two platforms' analytics from M4, so the publish and metrics layers are specified platform-agnostic.

D2 · V1 FORMATS — LOCKED

AI avatars are in, alongside slideshows and faceless hook videos. Constrained to keep the scope razor-thin: ~20 curated stock characters (not 1,000, no build-your-own until v2), previewed in the swipe feed as still + script, with the expensive lipsync render triggered only on right-swipe. Adds ~1 week to the build (now ~9 weeks).

D3 · TARGET USER — LOCKED

Solo app/SaaS builders — the category's proven wedge. The trend library, onboarding copy, and the first 15 discovery calls all target this niche.

D4 · MONETIZATION — LOCKED

Private beta → paid at launch, card required at signup. The first 500 customers enter a 30-day free window, then billing starts automatically; signups after the first 500 pay from day one. Stripe card-on-file, trial logic, and the first-500 counter ship in M4. Card-at-signup will shrink raw signup volume, but it filters for intent and makes trial→paid conversion a real metric from the first beta cohort. The 7-extra-days-for-a-call mechanic stays, granted as trial extensions.

D5 · NAME + PRICING — LOCKED

Product name: Man Friday (manfriday.app) — the dependable content sidekick; "Project Viral" stays the internal codename. Pricing rule: 0.7× the market leader. The leader anchors at $29 / $49 / $149; Man Friday ladders at $19 / $34 / $99, 20% off annual. The beta runs a single plan: Founding 500 → 30-day trial → $34/mo (the Growth-equivalent anchor). Margins hold at 0.7× because variable cost is credits, not seats: LLM spend is under $1/user/mo under determinism-first, and media credits (TTS, lipsync) are the margin dial — allowances sized so COGS stays under ~30% of price.

Scope is frozen. Next: the design phase — wireframes for the 5 screens, Convex schema, template spec, a platform-agnostic publish-layer contract covering TikTok + YouTube, both API applications, and the first 15 discovery calls. Build does not start until the design gate clears.
