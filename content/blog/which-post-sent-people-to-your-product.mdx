Here is a situation every founder who has posted for a month recognises. One video did 12,000 views. You
screenshot it. You post it in the group chat. And signups that week were… the same as always.

Meanwhile a plain little confession video, 1,900 views, was quietly the reason for the best Tuesday your
product ever had. You never found out, because nothing in the platform's dashboard connects a view to a
visit.

Views measure how well the platform's model liked your post. That is a useful number for the platform.
It is not the number you are optimising for.

## The number a founder needs

You are not a creator. You do not sell attention. You sell software, and the only thing a video can do for
you is send a person who was not thinking about your problem to a page where they can solve it.

So the question is never "how many people saw this?" It is:

> **Of the people who saw this, how many went to my product?**

That is a click, and clicks are measurable, if every post carries a link that only that post carries.

## How a tracked link works, in one paragraph

Every post Friday publishes gets its own short link, `manfriday.app/l/` plus seven characters, in the
caption. When someone taps it, the redirect logs one click with the platform it came from, then sends them
to your site with UTM tags so your own analytics see the visit too. Crawlers and link-preview bots are
recognised by their user agent and not counted. That is the entire mechanism. No pixel, no cookie, no
third-party script on your site.

One honest caveat: TikTok shows caption URLs as plain text, so the tap happens when someone copies it or
types it, or when the same post runs on Shorts where the link is live. Fewer clicks than views, always.
But the clicks you do get are real intent, which is the point.

## Read the ladder from the bottom

Every dashboard hands you a pile of numbers. Rank them by how close they sit to money:

- **Kept.** You liked the draft. Cheap to fake, because you are biased.
- **Published.** Table stakes. Consistency matters, but it is an input, not a result.
- **Views.** The platform's KPI. Necessary, noisy, and easily inflated by a hook that has nothing to do
  with your product.
- **Watch-through.** Closer. People who stay past the hook were interested in the thing, not just the line.
- **Click to your product.** The only signal you can bank. It is also the rarest, which is exactly why it
  is worth designing the post around.

When two posts disagree, the higher rung wins. A post with a third of the views and three times the clicks
is the better post, full stop, and the next batch should have more like it.

## What to do with the answer

Once you know which post sent people, the strategy writes itself, and it is short:

1. **Look at the hook pattern, not the topic.** The confession-then-turn structure works for founders
   because it is a story with a receipt at the end. If that pattern sent clicks, ask for more of that
   pattern about different parts of the product.
2. **Look at the format.** If the presenter video with your face out-clicked the faceless one, keep the
   face. If not, save yourself the mild discomfort.
3. **Look at the platform column.** If Shorts sends clicks and TikTok sends views, you have learned where
   your buyers actually live. Post there first.

The loop is a week long. Post, wait, read clicks, keep more of what clicked. After four loops you will know
things about your audience that no amount of "content strategy" reading can tell you.

## What we deliberately don't show

Friday's Analytics page shows clicks first, views last, and nothing else that would make you feel busy.
No follower graphs, no "engagement rate", no best-time-to-post heatmap. Those are creator metrics for
creator businesses. A founder needs one honest number per post and the discipline to act on it.
