Here's the pattern, and if you're a solo builder you've probably lived it.

You launch. You know distribution matters, so you commit: one TikTok a day. Week one goes
great — you have a backlog of ideas and launch adrenaline. Week two, you're writing hooks
at 11pm after a day of debugging. Week three, a customer issue eats your content slot.
Week four, your last post is nine days old and your streak is dead.

Then you conclude the wrong thing: *my content wasn't good enough.*

## The accounts that win aren't better. They're present.

Watch the small accounts that actually grow in the builder niche. Their videos are rarely
polished — most are text on a screenshot, a trending sound, a five-slide story. What they
have isn't taste. It's **presence**: they post every day, so the algorithm keeps testing
them, and one video in thirty catches. You can't catch if you're not in the water.

This is brutal math for a solo founder, because the required inputs are:

- **Volume** — platforms reward accounts that give them daily inventory.
- **Trend-reading** — knowing which format is working in your niche *this week*, not last month.
- **Turnaround** — a trend has a half-life of days; a video idea executed two weeks later is dead on arrival.

Every one of those is a daily job. You already have a daily job: the product.

## Doing both means doing both badly

The standard advice is "batch on Sundays." Batching solves volume and kills the other two —
you're posting Monday's read of the market all week. The other standard advice is "hire an
editor," which solves turnaround, costs real money pre-revenue, and still leaves you
writing every hook.

The honest version of the advice is: **posting is a role, not a task.** Companies staff it.
Solo builders can't — so the role either goes unfilled, or it cannibalizes the role only
you can do, which is building the thing people are supposed to find.
