If your product sells to people in Bangalore, São Paulo or Jakarta, you already know the uncomfortable
fact: the English version of your video is not really *for* them. It is for a global feed that happens to
include them. And the recommendation model knows it. Watch-through drops, clicks drop, and you conclude
short-form "does not work" in that market.

It works. It just has to sound like the market.

## Translation is not the feature

Every tool with a language dropdown does the same thing: take the English script, machine-translate it,
run it through a voice. The result is grammatically correct and instantly recognisable as foreign, because
nobody in Hyderabad talks in textbook Telugu about their SaaS, and nobody in Mumbai pitches a product in
pure Hindi.

They talk in **code-mixed** language. Hinglish. Tanglish. Tenglish. English nouns for the product world,
Hindi or Tamil for everything with feeling in it:

> "6 महीने तक ₹0 कमाया, फिर एक चीज़ बदली."

That is how the line lands. A translation of *"I made zero dollars for six months, then one thing
changed"* into formal Hindi does not land, and a Hindi speaker can tell within a syllable.

## What Friday does instead

The language lives on the brand and on every concept, not in a dropdown at the end:

- **Detect, then confirm.** Friday reads your site and proposes a language. You correct it with a chip, not
  a form. For the ten Indian languages there is a second control, *how it sounds*: code-mixed, pure native
  script, or Roman script for audiences that read Hindi on an English keyboard.
- **Rewrite, don't translate.** A kept English concept becomes a Hinglish one by writing the hook and the
  script again for that audience, with the same template shape. The number stays; the phrasing changes.
- **Voice that matches.** Indian languages are voiced by a model built for them, in the register you chose,
  by a speaker in your chosen voice. Captions are timed to the speech, in the right script, with the
  conjuncts drawn correctly. That last part sounds trivial and is where most rendering pipelines quietly
  fall apart.
- **One tap, one more video.** After you keep a concept, Friday offers the brand's other markets. Each one
  you add is a full video, counted as one on any plan. No "multi-language tier".

## The multiplier, honestly

A founder with one good idea a week and three markets has three posts a week, each native to its feed.
That is the whole pitch, and it is also where the caveats live:

- **The markets have to be real.** Do not add Tamil because it is offered. Add it because you have Tamil
  users, or want them, and can support them when they show up.
- **Code-mixed is a register, not a rule.** A fintech product pitched to a formal audience may want pure
  Hindi. Friday defaults to code-mixed because it is how most creators in those feeds actually talk, and
  lets you switch per brand.
- **Fourteen languages at launch, not forty.** English, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, Indonesian, and ten
  Indian languages: Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Kannada, Malayalam, Gujarati, Punjabi, Odia.
  Each one was tested end to end. A longer list would be marketing.

## Why this matters more for builders than for creators

A creator wants reach. A founder wants a customer in a specific place, and most of the world's next
hundred million software buyers do not scroll in English. Meeting them in their own register is not a
localisation chore. It is the distribution advantage a solo builder can have over a funded competitor
whose content team only writes in one language.
